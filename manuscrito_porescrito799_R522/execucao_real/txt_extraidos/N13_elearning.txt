ABSTRACT
This case study explores an innovative e-learning ecosystem for legal training in local 
government that integrates structured video lectures, interactive quizzes, and personalized 
microlearning powered by Micromate, an AI conversational learning assistant. Micromate 
complements complex legal topics—such as procurement law and housing benefit law—with 
accessible five-question chat-based microlearning sessions. The system uses generative AI to 
auto-create quiz sessions, analyzes individual forgetting curves, and adapts to each learner’s 
behavior. Five-question sessions fit seamlessly into daily routines, while gamification 
elements such as badges sustain motivation. Content is dynamically unlocked based on 
course progress, encouraging continuous engagement. Deployed across three pilot courses, 
Micromate achieved 70% voluntary participation among eligible staff, over 12,000 quiz 
questions answered during the six-month pilot phase across three e-learning-courses, and 
a 300% increase in weekly questions (800→2,400 from week 1 to 28). This comprehensive 
approach redefines professional development in public administration by creating efficient, 
engaging, and personalized learning experiences that enhance knowledge acquisition 
and retention
KEYWORDS
AI learning assistant, microlearning, conversational agent, public administration, legal 
education, spaced repetition, gamification

INTRODUCTION
Professional development in local government faces persistent challenges 
including traditional classroom-based legal training, difficulty retaining complex 
regulatory information, and limited time for extended learning sessions during 
busy work schedules. The legal domain presents particular challenges as statutory 
requirements are often perceived as dry and disconnected from daily work 
contexts, yet mastery is essential for regulatory compliance and effective public 
service delivery.

The vhw e-learning ecosystem with Micromate addresses these challenges by

MATERIALS AND METHODS
2.1 
System architecture and technical implementation
Micromate operates as a cloud-based Software-as-a-Service platform built on 
a sophisticated multi-tenant architecture. The core system integrates several key 
technical components designed for enterprise-grade deployment in public sector 
environments.
The conversational interface delivers learning through an integrated interface 
into the Moodle-based learning platform, eliminating the need for additional software 
installations or separate authentication systems. Users interact with Micromate as 
a chat contact while learning in the virtual classroom, creating a natural learning 
experience that feels like consulting with a knowledgeable colleague. The system 
supports seamless integration with existing Learning Management Systems through 
flexible APIs and can be embedded as web widgets in intranets, applications such as 
MS Teams, or deployed as standalone web applications [1].
Micromate’s adaptive learning algorithm continuously analyzes individual 
learning behavior including answer correctness, response time, learning frequency, 
and forgetting patterns to optimize question selection and timing. This spaced 
repetition methodology ensures knowledge consolidation through scientifically 
timed review cycles, adapting to each learner’s unique retention characteristics.
The system maintains full GDPR compliance with Swiss data residency, addressing 
critical public sector security and privacy requirements [2].
2.2 
Implementation timeline and methodology
The project progressed through five distinct phases from July 2023 to July 2025:
• System Familiarization & Plug-In Development (July 2023 – January 2024)
 
We began by exploring Micromate’s core functionality and integrating it into 
our Moodle-based LMS. Together with our cooperation partner, Micromate, 
we adapted the AI learning assistant to our environment and developed a custom 
Moodle plugin to enable seamless communication between Micromate and our 
course activities.
• Internal Testing (February 2024 – March 2024)
 
Ten internal colleagues helped us evaluate two content-packaging strategies: 
a single comprehensive learning package with tagged chapters versus multiple

• Pilot User Testing (June 2024)

We analyzed user activities and evaluated the feedback results collected via

RESULTS
The 6-hour e-learning course Basic knowledge of Procurement Law emerged 
as the clear frontrunner, achieving an engagement rate of 11.5%. This course 
dominated user interactions, accounting for 44% of all answered questions across 
the three-course pilot, making it the most successful implementation of the AI 
learning assistant. The Blended Learning course Facility Management completed the 
three-course evaluation, though specific engagement metrics were not detailed in 
the available data. The three courses combined covered 85% of all user interactions, 
demonstrating concentrated engagement within the pilot program [3].
Participation and Engagement Metrics:
• 70% of eligible staff voluntarily participated in at least one microlearning session, 
demonstrating strong learner buy-in without mandatory requirements
• Over 12,000 quiz questions were answered during the six-month pilot phase 
across all three courses
• 300% increase in weekly questions from 800 to 2,400 between week 1 and week 28
The evaluation noted missing precise session durations due to absent timestamps 
and unavailable completion rates, limiting comprehensive performance analysis. 
These metrics would be valuable for future optimization and benchmarking efforts.
The results indicate that procurement law content significantly outperformed 
facility management topics, suggesting either higher relevance to user needs or 
superior content design in legal domains. The sustained growth pattern demonstrates 
successful integration of AI-powered microlearning into daily work routines across 
all three courses.

DISCUSSION AND FUTURE IMPACT
The results suggest broad applicability beyond public administration, with 
potential for scaling across various professional domains requiring complex 
regulatory knowledge. Future research should focus on comparative effectiveness 
studies with traditional training methods, long-term knowledge retention validation, 
and optimization strategies for maximizing engagement across diverse content areas.
The integration of generative AI for automated content creation, combined with 
sophisticated spaced repetition algorithms and conversational interfaces, establishes 
a new paradigm for professional education that balances efficiency, effectiveness, and 
user experience in ways previously unattainable through conventional approaches.
5 
CONCLUSION
The Micromate AI-powered microlearning system represents a transformative 
approach to professional development in public administration. By combining 
conversational AI, adaptive learning algorithms, and evidence-based microlearning 
principles, the system achieved remarkable engagement rates and sustained 
participation growth. The 70% voluntary participation rate and 300% increase in 
weekly engagement demonstrate the system’s effectiveness in addressing traditional 
challenges of legal education in government settings.
This comprehensive approach enhances professional development by creating 
efficient, engaging, and personalized learning experiences that enhance knowledge 
acquisition and retention, establishing a new standard for AI-driven education in 
the public sector.
