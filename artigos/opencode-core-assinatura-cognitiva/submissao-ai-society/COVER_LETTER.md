# Cover Letter

**To:** The Editor-in-Chief, *AI & Society: Knowledge, Culture and Communication*

**Subject:** Submission of an Open Forum article — “The house that helps to think: authored cognitive prostheses, agentic AI, and the care of memory”

Dear Editor-in-Chief,

I am submitting the manuscript “The house that helps to think: authored cognitive prostheses, agentic AI, and the care of memory” for consideration as an **Open Forum** article in *AI & Society*.

The manuscript asks what happens when an AI system is designed by the person who uses it to compensate for a specific cognitive vulnerability — giftedness and a self-reported elevated risk of Alzheimer's disease — while also being designed to produce public, contestable evidence. It analyzes the OpenCode Ecosystem Core, a monorepo for agentic orchestration, memory externalization, specification, and verification. The article proposes the concept of the **authored cognitive prosthesis** and the notion of a **cognitive signature** as a situated configuration of documentable choices. It identifies five marks of that signature — memory treated as fragile, the contract before the impulse, verification against conviction, an architecture of interruption, and a didactic vocation — and examines the risks of dependency, overload, automation bias, and the bureaucratization of care. It closes with a pre-registered N-of-1 evaluation protocol with clinical oversight.

The manuscript speaks to *AI & Society* for three reasons. First, it treats AI as a societal and cognitive phenomenon, not only a technical one: it is concerned with technological mediation, care, authorship, accountability, and the distribution of cognitive resources. Second, it engages with foundational work in extended cognition, cognitive artifacts, distributed cognition, and cognitive reserve, and with contemporary work on agentic systems and evidence-based AI. Third, it offers a reflective, contextual contribution of the kind the journal invites — one that connects design choices to human consequences and to the question of who can build, use, and contest such systems.

The manuscript is a self-study. I am the creator and main user of the system. This relationship is disclosed in the manuscript, and the article treats it as a potential source of bias rather than as a claim to authority. The health information is a self-report, not a diagnosis. The article does not claim that the system prevents, treats, or diagnoses Alzheimer's disease or any other condition. The analysis is documentary; no clinical data or experimental outcomes are reported. The evaluation protocol is a proposal, not a result.

The manuscript was prepared with AI assistance for preliminary drafting, structural editing, and reference formatting. I reviewed, rewrote, and verified the text and take full responsibility for the content. A detailed statement is provided in the title page and in the manuscript's declarations. I am prepared to provide a fuller account of the human–AI division of labor if the editor wishes.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has not been published in its present form. An earlier, Portuguese-language version is available in the project's public repository; it is a preprint and is acknowledged in the title page. A public repository also contains the code and documentation; the URL is provided in the title page and withheld from the anonymized manuscript for double-blind review. The main text is approximately 7,500 words, excluding abstract, references, tables, and supplementary material.

I would be grateful for the opportunity to have the manuscript considered. I welcome the reviewers' criticism and will respond fully and promptly.
