# Cover Letter

**To:** The Editor-in-Chief, *Minds and Machines*

**Subject:** Submission of an Original Research article — “The house that helps to think: authored cognitive prostheses, agentic AI, and the limits of epistemic sovereignty”

Dear Editor-in-Chief,

I am submitting the manuscript “The house that helps to think: authored cognitive prostheses, agentic AI, and the limits of epistemic sovereignty” for consideration as an **Original Research** article in *Minds and Machines*.

The article addresses a philosophical puzzle at the intersection of philosophy of mind, epistemology, and AI ethics: when a person designs an agentic system to compensate for their own cognitive vulnerabilities, who authors the resulting cognition, and what kind of artifact is it? It develops a case study of the OpenCode Ecosystem Core, a monorepo for agentic orchestration, memory externalization, and verification, built by a researcher who reports giftedness and an elevated risk of Alzheimer's disease. The article defends a **moderate account of the cognitive prosthesis**: the system is not a literal part of the mind, but a robust scaffold that transforms the task, redistributes epistemic labor, and materializes a style of responsibility. It introduces the concept of the **cognitive signature** as a situated configuration of documentable choices, not a biomarker. It develops an **epistemology of the prosthesis**, centered on the difference between coordination devices and epistemic warrants, and on the virtue of institutionalized humility. It proposes an **ethics of self-design**, centered on care, epistemic inequality, and contestability. It responds to five objections, including the charge that the case is merely a personal knowledge management system and the charge that a self-study cannot support philosophical claims.

The manuscript speaks to *Minds and Machines* for three reasons. First, it engages directly with the journal's core concerns: the role of digital technologies in understanding cognitive processes, the epistemic status of computer science and AI, the moral status and responsibility of artificial agents, and the ethical and social implications of design. Second, it brings together debates that are often kept apart: the extended mind debate (Clark & Chalmers, Rupert, Sterelny, Adams & Aizawa), cognitive artifacts (Norman), distributed cognition (Hutchins, Hollan et al.), epistemic dependence (Hardwig), epistemic injustice (Fricker), care ethics (Tronto), and the morality of artificial agents (Floridi & Sanders). Third, it is a philosophical case study, not an engineering report: the technical details are subordinated to the argument, and the case is used to illuminate a phenomenon and generate hypotheses, not to establish a general law.

The manuscript is a self-study. I am the creator and main user of the system. This relationship is disclosed, and the article treats it as a potential source of bias rather than as a claim to authority. The health information is a self-report, not a diagnosis. The article does not claim that the system prevents, treats, or diagnoses Alzheimer's disease or any other condition. The analysis is documentary; no clinical data or experimental outcomes are reported. The N-of-1 protocol is a proposal, not a result.

The manuscript was prepared with AI assistance for preliminary drafting, structural editing, and reference formatting. I reviewed, rewrote, and verified the text and take full responsibility for the content. A detailed statement is provided in the title page and in the manuscript's declarations. The use of AI is also part of the article's object of reflection: the article examines a system that itself uses AI agents, and my own use of AI is disclosed rather than hidden.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has not been published in its present form. An earlier, Portuguese-language version is available as a public preprint; it is acknowledged in the title page. The main text is approximately 8,000 words, excluding abstract, references, tables, and supplementary material.

I would be grateful for the opportunity to have the manuscript considered. I welcome the reviewers' criticism and will respond fully and promptly.
