# International edition: plan and English abstract {#international}

> **Status:** this is a **plan and a sample**, not a published translation. The
> Portuguese edition is the reference; the English edition requires
> translation, copyediting, peer review and a publisher.

## 1. Why an international edition

The book's unique contribution is not “another quantum computing textbook”. It
is the **integration** of:

- Bohm's interpretation and dialogue as a pedagogical lens;
- three levels (N0–N2) and ten lenses per concept;
- manual practice before code;
- Python and real hardware;
- hand calculations and footnote derivations;
- evidence cards with DOIs;
- Lean 4 verification of algebraic identities;
- open license and self-contained PDF.

No major international textbook combines all of these. Nielsen & Chuang is the
reference for depth; Qiskit Textbook for hands-on coding; Bernhardt for
accessibility; Bohm & Hiley for ontology. This book is the **bridge**.

## 3. English abstract

> *From Bit to Whole* is an open, self-contained introduction to quantum information for
> readers who have never studied the subject — and for those who already have.
> It is organized as 100 concepts, each seen through ten lenses (idea, analogy,
> practice, laboratory, common error, formal depth, dialogue, ethics, life and
> checkpoint) and revisited at three levels: N0 (no mathematics), N1 (circuits,
> tables and short formulas) and N2 (formalization, statistical limits and
> replication). The through-line is David Bohm's emphasis on wholeness,
> dialogue and the implicate order, presented as an interpretation among
> others, not as a confirmed theory.
>
> The book combines manual experiments (cards, coins, dice), Python simulations
> (Qiskit, PennyLane, Cirq), and real hardware runs on Tiangong/Guoji08 and
> Baihua, with dates and task IDs. Every quantitative claim is tied to a count,
> a calculation, a DOI or a formal proof. Algebraic identities used in the
> text — H² = I, HZH = X, the classical CHSH bound S ≤ 2, the identity
> (2√2)² = 8, the CNOT truth table and probability normalization — are
> machine-checked in Lean 4 + Mathlib. The book is explicit about what Lean
> does not prove: hardware counts, calibration, fidelity, noise and the Bohmian
> interpretation remain empirical or interpretive claims.
>
> The Portuguese edition has 3580 pages, 3000 lauds, 400 calculation notes, 100
> evidence cards, 18 experiments, 9427 internal links and 54 tests. It is
> released under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; the code is MIT. The English edition is a
> translation and peer-review project, not a promise of acceptance or adoption.

## 6. Translation plan

1. **Sample:** translate the architecture chapter, the trails chapter and one
   concept (Bit) at all three levels.
2. **Review:** send the sample to 3 reviewers (physics education, quantum
   computing, Bohm scholar).
3. **Glossary:** build a PT–EN glossary of 100 concepts, with Bohm terms
   (implicate order, holomovement, pilot wave, dialogue).
4. **Full translation:** 12–18 months, 2 translators + 1 technical editor.
5. **Technical check:** run all 18 experiments with the English code; compare
   outputs.
6. **Copyedit:** 2 passes; consistency of notation, units and references.
7. **Layout:** light theme, 16 × 23 cm, 600–800 pages.
8. **Peer review:** 3 external reviewers; response letter.
9. **Publication:** DOI, ISBN, print and open PDF.

- a controlled efficacy study;
- a lean print edition;
- a DOI and a stable repository;
- an English edition with technical review;
- errata and a living community.

Without these, the book is a strong **8/10 national didactic reference** and a
**6.5–7/10 international candidate**, not a 10/10 international textbook.

## 9. Sample: how to read this book (English)

> This book does not ask you to believe it. It asks you to cross it, one page at
> a time, and check. Each page is a *laud*: one concept, one lens, one level,
> seven minutes of active reading. The same concept returns ten times — as an
> idea, an analogy, a practice, a laboratory, a common error, a formal
> definition, a dialogue, an ethical question, a life connection and a
> checkpoint. It returns three times: without mathematics (N0), with circuits
> and short formulas (N1), and with formalization and statistical limits (N2).
>
> You do not need to read 3000 pages linearly. Start with the 30-day trail, then
> choose a level. The book is a curriculum, not a race.
