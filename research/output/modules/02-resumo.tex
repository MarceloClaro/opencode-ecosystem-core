\section*{Abstract}

\textbf{Background}: Algorithmic bias in healthcare artificial intelligence 
threatens equitable care delivery. Diabetes prediction models may 
systematically disadvantage underrepresented populations, exacerbating 
existing health disparities.

\textbf{Methods}: We evaluated four machine learning classifiers (Logistic 
Regression, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, SVM) on the Pima Indians 
Diabetes Database (768 samples, 8 features) using 5-fold stratified 
cross-validation. Performance was assessed across demographic subgroups 
defined by gender and ethnicity. Fairness was quantified using Equalized 
Odds and Demographic Parity metrics. Intersectional analysis examined bias 
at the intersection of gender and ethnicity.

\textbf{Results}: The SVM model achieved the best performance (F1: 0.8313, 
AUC-ROC: 0.8794, CV: 0.7859±0.0229). All models exhibited bias, with 
Equalized Odds differences ranging from 0.0306 to 0.1143 across groups. 
False positive rates were consistently higher for minority patients 
(0.333--0.417) compared to majority patients (0.164--0.200). Intersectional 
analysis revealed amplified bias for minority females (TPR: 0.714, F1: 0.750).

\textbf{Conclusions}: Significant algorithmic bias exists across demographic 
groups in diabetes prediction models. Minority females experience compounded 
disadvantage at the intersection of multiple marginalized identities. 
Fairness-aware development, continuous monitoring, and transparent reporting 
are essential for equitable clinical AI deployment.

\textbf{Keywords}: algorithmic bias, diabetes, equity, machine learning, 
underrepresented populations, intersectionality, public health.
