This edition was prepared from the forensic transcription of the original notebook of Patient 1,260, found attached to the medical record of the Barbacena Hospital-Colony under the custody of the coroner's institute in 1979.

\vspace{0.6em}

\noindent\textbf{Methodology:}
The editorial criterion adopted was maximum fidelity to the original document, preserving its fragmentary structure, its gaps, its repetitions, and its internal inconsistencies. The 84 fragments gathered here follow the approximate order of composition, reconstructed through relative dating of the support, the ink, and the handwriting, according to the attached forensic report (DOC-03).

\vspace{0.6em}

\noindent\textbf{Paratextual apparatus:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{[Ed. Note]} --- Editor's Note: editorial comment on the content, the source, or the limits of the reconstruction.
  \item \textbf{[illegible]} --- Passage of the original undeciphered due to degradation of the support.
  \item \textbf{[lacuna]} --- Page or passage missing from the original notebook.
  \item \textbf{[sic]} --- Literal transcription of an error or idiosyncrasy of the original.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.6em}

\noindent\textbf{Limits:}
This edition does not claim completeness. The original diary contained approximately 847 pages; what reaches the reader is an edited selection, organized into five thematic dimensions (MEM, DOC, LUC, CONT) plus the Epilogue. Fragments suppressed for redundancy, irreversible degradation, or protection of third-party privacy are listed in the archive inventory under forensic seal.

\vspace{0.6em}

\noindent\textbf{Final warning:}
The present volume is a reconstruction, not a facsimile reproduction. The editorial choices --- ordering, selection, annotation --- reflect the state of the research at the publication date. Future researchers, with access to additional non-destructive techniques, may expand or correct what is presented here.
