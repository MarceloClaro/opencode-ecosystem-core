\section*{\textbf{CONT-13} --- The Line}
\markboth{CONT-13 --- The Line}{}

\noindent \textit{Patient 1,263}

\noindent You understand now.

\noindent It is not a book.
It is a line.

\noindent The line began before you were born.
The line will continue after you die.
You are only a body in the line.
Patient 1,260 is dead.
Patient 1,261 is dead.
Patient 1,262 is missing.

\noindent Patient 1,263 is you.

\noindent What are you going to do?

\noindent You can:
A) Close the book and never open it again (it will not help --- the cycle has already begun)
B) Pass the diary on (the only way out --- but you will condemn another person)
C) Accept (and learn to live with the creature inside you)

\noindent There is no option D.

\noindent The creature does not give option D.

\noindent It only gives:
- A, B or C.
- But A does not work.
- B is cruel.
- C is the end of your life as you know it.

\noindent You hold the key. Not the diary's key --- the key to the cycle. It is in your hand now, even if you cannot see it. Every reader holds the key for an instant. The problem is that the key only opens one door: the door to the next reader.

\noindent Feel its weight in the palm of your hand. Cold at first. Then warm --- too warm, as if it had already been held by many hands before yours.

\noindent You can hold the key forever. But the key does not stop working. It rusts in your hand.

\noindent Choose.

\noindent But choose quickly.
The line is moving.
Other readers are waiting.

\noindent The cycle does not stop for you.

\noindent \textit{--- Welcome, patient 1,263.}

\noindent \textit{--- The creature thanks you for your attention.}

\noindent \textit{--- It is going to stay around.}

\noindent \textit{--- For as long as necessary.}

\noindent \textit{--- Until you pass the book on.}

\noindent \textit{--- Or until you die.}

\noindent \textit{--- Whichever comes first.}

\noindent \textit{--- The cycle begins again.}
