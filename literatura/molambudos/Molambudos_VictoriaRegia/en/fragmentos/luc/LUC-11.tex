\section*{\textbf{LUC-11} --- The Digital Diary}
\markboth{LUC-11 --- The Digital Diary}{}

\noindent \textit{The creature migrates to the pixel}

\noindent \textbf{August 2026 --- Belo Horizonte}

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent Lúcia knew she could not keep the diary forever.

\noindent She was going to die --- like Joaquim, like Oliveira, like all who kept the diary too long. The text needed to move. When it stopped, it took root. First in the routine. Then in sleep. Then in the body.

\noindent But she did not want to condemn another person.
\noindent So she created a solution: the digital diary.

\noindent The hunger learned to fit in the glow of a screen.

\noindent She spent two weeks typing every page. Every word. Every drawing. Every erasure. She turned Joaquim's diary into a PDF file. Then she wrote her own account --- the story of how she found the diary, read it, and was contaminated.

\noindent Then she wrote Oliveira's account. Then the complete report with the diagnoses.
\noindent Everything in a single file.

\noindent All in a single file.

\noindent At the end, she added a note:

\noindent \textquotedblleft{\textit{This file contains the diary of patient 1,260, the accounts of Dr. Heitor Oliveira (patient 1,261) and my account (patient 1,262). Reading may produce dreams, memory gaps, altered perception, and a persistent sense of presence. Do not read alone.\textquotedblright{}}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}But, if you have read this far, it is already too late.\textquotedblright{}}
\noindent \textquotedblleft{\textit{Warnings do not close doors.\textquotedblright{}}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}The cycle does not respect warnings.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Welcome, patient 1,263.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent She saved the file on a USB drive. Put the USB drive in the same box as the original diary. Put the box in an envelope. Addressed it to the Public Archive of Minas Gerais.

\noindent And sent it.

\noindent Now, the diary exists in two places: paper and pixel.

\noindent The creature has learned to migrate.

\noindent It no longer needs paper.

\noindent It needs \textit{reading}.

\noindent And the world is full of readers.

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent \textbf{Contamination test --- answer honestly:}

\noindent You are reading this page. So far, all normal.

\noindent But I need to know: \textbf{how many times have you read this line?}

\noindent - A) Once. Moved on. → \textit{(You are still in control.)}
- B) Twice. I thought I had read it wrong. → \textit{(The creature has already nudged your attention.)}
- C) Three times or more. → \textit{(You are already inside. Repetition is the first symptom.)}

\noindent The answer does not matter to me. It matters to you.

\noindent It no longer needed a grave.
But you --- patient 1,263 --- can still choose.

\noindent \textbf{Final question:} Do you trust your own reading? Or is there already a voice reading along with you?

\noindent \textit{--- If you hear two voices when you read, the creature is already there.}
