\section*{\textbf{LUC-04} --- The Photograph}
\markboth{LUC-04 --- The Photograph}{}

\noindent \textit{The old man in the cemetery}

\noindent \textbf{March 2026 --- Barbacena}

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent Lúcia returned to the hotel at 10 PM.
\noindent The air came in thick. For a second, she had the absurd impression that she was not breathing: she was being breathed.

\noindent The hotel was a simple inn in the center of Barbacena. Small room, hard bed, a window facing the main street. She put her backpack on the bed, took out the diary and the notebook, and sat down.

\noindent The diary was on the bed.

\noindent She still had not opened it.

\noindent In the two weeks since she had picked up the diary at the Public Archive, she had not managed to open it. There was always an excuse: work, fatigue, the wrong time. The truth was that she was afraid.

\noindent Not rational fear.
Fear of what she would find.
Fear of what she would feel.
\noindent Not a rational fear. Fear of what she would find. Fear of what she would feel. Fear of what would change inside her.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}You are a forensic psychiatrist\textquotedblright{}\textit{, she said out loud. }\textquotedblleft{}You deal with criminals, with traumatized people, with archives of horror. This is just another case.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent She opened the diary.

\noindent The first page had Joaquim's childish handwriting. The date: 1917.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Today I arrived at the Colony. The doctor said I am crazy. But I am not crazy. The creature is the one who is crazy. She lives inside me and talks all the time.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent Lúcia read.

\noindent Page after page.

\noindent The childhood in the Sertão. The drought. The walk. The father dead at the juazeiro tree. The mother taken by the soldiers. The government pen. The trench.

\noindent She read about the creature.

\noindent She read the description of the body of spines, of the ribs turned outward, of the pulsing organ. She read about the light trapped in the eye. She read about the deal.

\noindent She read about the yellow eye. She read about the deal.

\noindent When she raised her eyes, it was 3:14 in the morning.

\noindent The room was dark. The only light came from the lamp on the bedside table. The diary was open to page 126.
\noindent She felt the rotten sweetness.

\noindent She smelled it.

\noindent Sweetish.

\noindent It was in the room.

\noindent She looked at the corner. The shadow seemed denser there. Darker. As if the dark were darker in that patch of wall.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}There is nothing there\textquotedblright{}}, she said.

\noindent But she did not believe it.

\noindent She closed the diary. Put it in her backpack. Lay down. Turned off the light.

\noindent She lay staring at the ceiling.

\noindent At 3:14 in the morning, the yellow eye appeared in the corner of the room.

\noindent It was not a dream.

\noindent She saw it.

\noindent She blinked.

\noindent The eye was gone.

\noindent She spent the rest of the night with the light on, the diary closed under her pillow, and the certainty that something had entered the room.
\noindent The certainty that something had entered \textit{her}.

\noindent Something had signed for her.

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent The next morning, she went back to the Colony.

\noindent This time, she went to the burial ground. The hospital cemetery, where sixty thousand bodies were buried in common graves. There were no crosses. There were no names. Only a smooth lawn, as if nothing were buried there.

\noindent There were no crosses. No names. Only a smooth lawn, as if nothing were buried there.

\noindent She knew Joaquim was there. Grave 1,260.

\noindent She stood still, looking at the ground.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}What do you want from me?\textquotedblright{}}, she asked out loud.

\noindent The wind answered with silence.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Why me?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent A dry leaf spun at her feet.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}You read it\textquotedblright{}}, said a voice behind her.

\noindent Lúcia turned around.

\noindent An old man was standing a few meters away. Very old. Wrinkled skin, thin white hair, dull eyes. He held a cane.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Who are you?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Just an old man who passed by here\textquotedblright{}\textit{, he said. }\textquotedblleft{}And who saw you reading the diary.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}How do you know I read it?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent He smiled. His mouth was a crooked line.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}The smell. You have the smell. Whoever read the diary keeps the smell for days.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}What smell?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Sweet. Of wilted flower. Of gone-off meat.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent Lúcia felt her stomach tighten.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Who are you?\textquotedblright{}} she repeated.

\noindent The old man came closer. Slowly. His cane tapping on the dry ground.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}I knew patient 1,260.\textquotedblright{}\textit{ }\textquotedblleft{}Liar. He died in '79.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}I knew him before. I worked here in the 50s.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}What do you know about him?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent The old man stopped in front of her.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}I know he wrote in a diary. I know the diary was buried with him. I know that, when they exhumed him, the diary was warm. And I know that everyone who read the diary...\textquotedblright{}\textit{ He paused. }\textquotedblleft{}...had a visit.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}What visit?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}The Shroud-Ripper.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent The old man pointed at the sky.

\noindent Lúcia looked.

\noindent On the dry branch of a tree, a white owl was perched. It stared fixedly at Lúcia.

\noindent \textquotedblleft{\textit{She comes when someone opens the diary\textquotedblright{}}, said the old man. }\textquotedblleft{\textit{She watches until the person chooses.\textquotedblright{}}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}What cycle?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Patient 1,260's cycle. He dies, someone reads, the reader becomes the next one.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent The old man turned his back.

\noindent \textquotedblleft{\textit{Wait!\textquotedblright{}} Lúcia called. }\textquotedblleft{\textit{How do I stop this?\textquotedblright{}}}

\noindent He stopped. He did not turn his face.
\noindent \textquotedblleft{\textit{It does not stop. It changes hands. You close yours and another opens. That is how a buried thing learns to walk.\textquotedblright{}}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}You do not.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}I do not?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}You only pass it on. Find another reader. Hand over the diary. Let someone else read it. Then the cycle continues. Only with someone else.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent He started walking.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}And if I do not pass it on?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent The old man's voice came from afar, carried by the wind:

\noindent \textquotedblleft{\textit{Then you rot with it inside you. Like Joaquim. Like the doctor. Like all who thought they could keep a closed door in their chest.\textquotedblright{}}}

\noindent The white owl spread its wings. Flew.

\noindent Lúcia was left alone in the cemetery, with the diary in her backpack, the smell in her nose, and the certainty that something had changed forever.
