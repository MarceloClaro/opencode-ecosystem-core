\section*{\textbf{LUC-08} --- Lúcia's Last Session}
\markboth{LUC-08 --- Lúcia's Last Session}{}

\noindent \textit{The psychiatrist becomes the patient}

\noindent \textbf{May 2026 --- Lúcia's office, Belo Horizonte}

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent She booked a session with a psychiatrist.

\noindent Not as a professional --- as a patient.

\noindent Dr. Regina Alves had been Lúcia's friend since medical school. She worked in an elegant office in Belo Horizonte, with leather armchairs and a view of the mountains. Lúcia sat in the patient's chair for the first time in 15 years.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}What is happening, Lúcia?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}I am afraid I am going crazy.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Why?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}I am having psychotic symptoms.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Which ones?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent Lúcia listed them: the smell, the dreams, the yellow eye, the feeling of not being alone, her handwriting changing.

\noindent Regina listened in silence. She took notes.

\noindent After 20 minutes, she asked:

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Is there something else?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}...There is a diary.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent She told her about the case of patient 1,260. About the creature. About the cycle.

\noindent When she finished, Regina closed her notebook.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Lúcia, you are presenting a picture of acute stress. Possibly a dissociative disorder triggered by contact with traumatic material.\textquotedblright{}}
\noindent \textquotedblleft{\textit{You think it is suggestion?\textquotedblright{}}}

\noindent \textquotedblleft{\textit{What do you think is happening?\textquotedblright{}}}
\noindent \textquotedblleft{\textit{I think someone assembled an archive to produce a specific reaction in whoever read it. Or I think the archive found a way to keep being read. Both hypotheses frighten me the same way.\textquotedblright{}}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}I think you read a disturbing case, your brain processed the trauma, and now it is producing symptoms compatible with what you read. It is an identification mechanism.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}And the eye?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}What about the eye?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent Lúcia took off her sunglasses.

\noindent The right eye was yellow.

\noindent Regina tilted her head.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}That... is new?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}It appeared after I read the diary.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Have you seen an ophthalmologist?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}I did. He said there is no explanation.\textquotedblright{}}
\noindent \textquotedblleft{\textit{Lúcia, do you believe a story can cause a physiological change in the eye?\textquotedblright{}}}

\noindent Regina fell silent.

\noindent It could be the lantern. It could be mica in the clay.
\noindent Lúcia chose to believe it was the lantern --- even after 47 years.
\noindent \textquotedblleft{\textit{I believe repeated exposure to traumatic material can produce real symptoms.\textquotedblright{}}}
\noindent \textquotedblleft{\textit{It is the only honest answer I have.\textquotedblright{}}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}No.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}But it is happening.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}It is.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}What do you think it is?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent Lúcia took a deep breath.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}I think what is in the diary is real. I think the creature exists. I think it passed from Joaquim to Oliveira. And from Oliveira to me.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent Regina did not answer.

\noindent After a long silence, she said:

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Lúcia, as a friend, I ask you: seek treatment. Hospitalization, if necessary.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}And as a psychologist?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}As a psychologist...\textquotedblright{}\textit{ Regina hesitated. }\textquotedblleft{}As a psychologist, I ask you: what are you going to do with this diagnosis?\textquotedblright{}}
\noindent Lúcia took a sealed envelope from her bag.

\noindent Lúcia stood up.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}I am going to write. I am going to record everything. So that, when the cycle reaches the next person, she will know what is happening.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}And if the next person is me?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent Lúcia stopped at the door.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Then you will understand.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent She left.

\noindent She never spoke to Regina again.

\noindent Three weeks later, she received an envelope by mail. No note. No sender.
\noindent Inside was a small key, still damp, and a page torn from a diary Regina had never seen.

\noindent She put it all in a drawer.
\noindent At least that is what she declared afterward.

\noindent She did not open it.

\noindent She did not read it.

\noindent To this day.
