\section*{\textbf{DOC-11} --- Oliveira's Diary I}
\markboth{DOC-11 --- Oliveira's Diary I}{}

\noindent \textit{The first symptom}

\noindent \textbf{Dr. Heitor Oliveira --- Personal Notebook}
\textbf{April 15, 1979}

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent \textit{Note: This notebook was found in Dr. Oliveira's locker after his disappearance. The first pages are written in a firm, doctor's hand. The last ones are almost illegible. The following transcription is literal.}

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent Day 1.

\noindent The hospital administration gave me a week of leave. They said I \textquotedblleft{}needed to rest\textquotedblright{}. What they mean is that they need time to decide whether I am crazy.

\noindent I am not crazy.

\noindent But I am afraid.

\noindent The fear is professional: I have begun having symptoms I diagnose in my patients. Insomnia. Hypervigilance. Intrusive thoughts. The face of patient 1,260 appears in my mind when I close my eyes. The yellow eye.

\noindent Specifically the eye.

\noindent It glows.

\noindent That is not a metaphor. I saw it. In the cell, that night, the patient's eye glowed in the dark like a cat's eye. There is no physiological explanation. There is no disease that causes this.

\noindent I tried to reason: pigment accumulation, anomalous retinal reflection, a rare variant of tapetum lucidum in humans. No. There is no record. There is no literature. There is nothing.

\noindent The eye glows.

\noindent And now, when I close my eyes, I see it.

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent Day 2.

\noindent I woke up at 3:14 in the morning. The same hour patient 1,260 says he wakes every night. I got up, went to the bathroom, washed my face.

\noindent When I looked in the mirror, my right eye seemed lighter.

\noindent I stood looking for 5 minutes. Blinking. Opening and closing. The bathroom light is white, strong. There is no shadow. There is no illusion.

\noindent The iris of the right eye is a lighter shade than that of the left.

\noindent This is impossible. Eye color does not change in adulthood. I feel I am losing my mind.

\noindent But I am not.

\noindent I am recording.

\noindent I am documenting.

\noindent Because, if I am going mad, I want someone to read this and know \textit{how} it happened.

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent Day 3.

\noindent The smell.

\noindent I woke up and the room was full of a sweetish smell. Like sweet meat. Like a wilted flower. The same smell I felt in patient 1,260's cell.

\noindent I got up, opened the window. The outside air was clean, cold, normal. But inside the room, the smell persisted.

\noindent It was on the pillow. On the sheet. On my skin.

\noindent I took a shower. I scrubbed my skin with soap. The smell went away --- but it came back minutes later. As if it were coming from inside me.

\noindent I washed the bedding. The smell went away.

\noindent When I lay down, the smell came back.

\noindent It is not the environment. It is me.

\noindent I am exuding the smell.

\noindent I ran an experiment: I asked my wife to smell my arm. She said: \textquotedblleft{}I smell nothing.\textquotedblright{} So is it subjective? Olfactory? Hallucinatory?

\noindent I asked her to smell again, carefully. She said: \textquotedblleft{}There is a smell... strange? Sweet? Now that you mention it, maybe.\textquotedblright{}

\noindent She felt it.

\noindent It is not hallucination.

\noindent It is contagion.

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent Day 4.

\noindent I dreamed of the trench.

\noindent I was lying on the pile of bodies. Their eyes were open. The creature was on top of me. Her face close to mine. She said:

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}You read. Now you are mine.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent I woke up screaming.

\noindent My wife sleeps on the sofa now. She said I talk in my sleep. I asked what I say. She did not want to repeat it. I insisted.

\noindent She said: \textquotedblleft{}You say: 'The cycle begins again. Patient 1,261.'\textquotedblright{}

\noindent Patient 1,261.

\noindent The number after 1,260.

\noindent \textbf{Me.}

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent Day 5.

\noindent I went to the hospital. I entered the ward of the silenced. I stood at the door of cell 1,260. The patient is no longer there --- they transferred him to the general ward after the sessions stopped.

\noindent But the cell is empty.

\noindent I went in.

\noindent The sweetish smell is strong here. Stronger than anywhere else.

\noindent On the floor, near the bed, there was a piece of paper. I picked it up. It was written, in tiny handwriting:

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}The doctor will come back. He has already dreamed of the trench. He has already felt the smell. He is already mine.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent The date on the paper: 1919.

\noindent Written 60 years ago.
Foreseeing I would be here.
Foreseeing everything.

\noindent I ran out of the cell. I went down the corridor. The patients stared. Some laughed. Others screamed.

\noindent One of them, an old man in the last cell, said as I passed:

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Welcome, Doctor. The creature was waiting for you.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent I stopped.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}What did you say?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}She said. She said you would come. She said you would feel the smell. She said you would dream.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Who said?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}The Shroud-Ripper.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent He pointed at the window.

\noindent On the sill, a white owl was looking at me.

\noindent When I blinked, it was gone.

\noindent But the 1919 note is in my pocket.

\noindent And the handwriting --- the handwriting is changing.

\noindent My handwriting.

\noindent It is becoming like his.
