\markboth{DOC-19 --- Brazilian Holocaust}{}

\noindent \textit{60 thousand dead at the Colony}

\noindent \textbf{Excerpt from the book \textit{Brazilian Holocaust} --- Daniela Arbex (2013)}
\textbf{Authorized transcription for historical context purposes}

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}The Colony was the largest asylum in Brazil. In its 80 years of existence, it is estimated that 60 thousand patients died within its walls. In the 1960s alone, 15 patients died per day.}

\noindent \textit{The bodies were buried in common graves, without identification. Often, patients were buried alive --- over-sedated, they were taken for dead and interred. When the graves were opened for exhumation, skeletons were found in positions indicating that they had moved after being buried.}

\noindent \textit{Of the 60 thousand dead, fewer than 3 thousand have an official death record. The remaining 57 thousand simply 'disappeared'.\textquotedblright{}}\NE{Daniela Arbex's book (\textit{Brazilian Holocaust}, 2013) documents 1,853 bodies exhumed from the trenches of the Hospital-Colony and estimates 60 thousand dead in 80 years of operation. Joaquim Antônio Correia, Patient 1,260, is a fictional character; the 60 thousand dead of the Colony are historical. This note exists so that the distinction does not fade in the immersion of reading. Real horror does not need fiction to be true; fiction must not parasitize real horror without marking it.}

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent \textbf{Archivist's note (2026):}

\noindent Patient 1,260, Joaquim Antônio Correia, is one of the 60 thousand.

\noindent His body was exhumed in 1979. The diary found with him is the only written record of a Colony patient that survived the abandonment.

\noindent The question that remains is: how much of what he wrote is delusion --- and how much is testimony?

\noindent This Archive takes no position.
