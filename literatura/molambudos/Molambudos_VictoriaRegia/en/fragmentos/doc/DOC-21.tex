\section*{\textbf{DOC-21} --- Letter from Heitor Oliveira (unsent)}
\markboth{DOC-21 --- Letter from Heitor Oliveira (unsent)}{}

\noindent \textit{Document extracted from the forensic file.}

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent {\footnotesize\texttt{PUBLIC ARCHIVE $\cdot$ DOCUMENTARY EVIDENCE $\cdot$ CASE 1,260}}\\[-0.2em]

\noindent \textbf{Addressee:} Maria Oliveira (wife).\\
\noindent \textbf{Sender:} Dr. Heitor Oliveira.\\
\noindent \textbf{Place:} Barbacena.\\
\noindent\textbf{Date:} not recorded (between June 16 and June 20, 1979).\\
\noindent\textbf{State of the original:} found inside the back cover of Dr. Oliveira's personal notebook, folded in four, without an envelope. It was never sent.

\noindent Maria,

\noindent If you are reading this, I have already left home. Or I have left
\noindent myself. I do not know which
\noindent happened first.

\noindent The 1,260's diary is with me. I did not ask permission to remove it
\noindent from the IML. I signed the register book as if I had authorization, and no one
\noindent questioned it. That is how it works here: a doctor signs, no one asks.

\noindent I thought I would settle it in two days. Pick up the notebook, read it calmly,
\noindent write an assessment, return it. Routine stuff. The man had been dead for
\noindent sixty-two years. What more could happen?

\noindent Maria, I did not read the diary. The diary read me.

\noindent On the third day, I woke up with a yellow stain on my eye. It does not hurt.
\noindent It does not itch. But it is growing. The ophthalmologist said it is a
\noindent subconjunctival hemorrhage. It will disappear in two weeks. But I know it is not
\noindent blood. It is the color of the eye the patient describes on page 40. I reread
\noindent page 40 this morning. The description is identical to the stain on my eye.

\noindent On the fifth day, I felt the smell.

\noindent You know the smell of the Colony. You came with me once, in 1975.
\noindent You said you would never come back. Remember? The smell of creolin, of feces, of
\noindent reheated food, of closed-up bodies. The diary has that smell. But it did not come
\noindent from the Colony. It came from the paper. As if the ink had been made with
\noindent something that rotted and was forgotten.

\noindent On the seventh day, I began to write.

\noindent It was not me writing. It was the hand. It moved on its own, just like the
\noindent patient describes. I watched the handwriting come out --- and it was not mine. It was
\noindent his. The same slant to the left. The same open \textquotedblleft{}a\textquotedblright{}. The same
\noindent irregular pressure on the paper, as if the pen were too heavy.

\noindent Today I woke up and did not know where I was. I woke up inside a
\noindent memory that was not mine. A barbed-wire fence. The sun burning
\noindent the back of my neck. People too thin leaning against the wire, with still eyes. I
\noindent never saw that. But the body remembered. The hands trembled as if they had been
\noindent holding hot wire.

\noindent Maria, I am afraid to sleep. Every time I close my eyes, I
\noindent go back to the trench. It is the same trench he describes. The same arms coming out
\noindent of the earth. The same sweet smell that will not leave my nose.

\noindent I am not going to return the diary.

\noindent I know it sounds like madness. But if I return it, someone will read it. And if
\noindent someone reads it, the same thing will happen. I cannot do that to
\noindent anyone.

\noindent Or --- I do not know if I am protecting others or if the diary does not
\noindent want to be returned.

\noindent It likes me.

\noindent It likes being with me.

\noindent It does not want to go back to the drawer.

\noindent I am sorry.

\noindent Heitor

\noindent Archivist's note: the letter was found in 2026, inside Dr. Oliveira's personal notebook, returned to the Minas Gerais Public Archive together with patient 1,260's diary, 47 years after it was written. The fold of the paper suggests he carried it with him for all that time.
