\section*{\textbf{MEM-01} --- The Sertão Before the End}
\markboth{MEM-01 --- The Sertão Before the End}{}


\noindent \textbf{1914 --- Quixeramobim, Ceará}

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent My father used to say the land has a way of warning you.

\noindent First the cattle die. They feel it before we do. The cattle start to fall --- not from disease, from weakness. The pasture disappears, the cattle grow thin, the bones show through the hide, and one day the ox lies down and does not get up again. My father slaughtered the ones still standing so as not to lose the meat. But there was no water to salt it. The meat rotted before we could eat it.

\noindent After the cattle, the river dries up. The Quixeramobim was a river that sang. I could hear it from the doorway of our house --- a sound of water over stones, low, like someone talking to himself. In 1914, it stopped singing. First it grew shallow. Then it turned muddy. Then it ran dry. The riverbed looked like a road of white bone.

\noindent After the river, people change.

\noindent My father changed first. He began to sit for hours on the doorstep, staring at nothing. My mother asked him what was wrong. He would say: \textit{\textquotedblleft{}I'm waiting.\textquotedblright{}} He never said for what. I think he did not know himself.

\noindent Then the neighbors changed. Seu Lázaro, the happiest man in the region, stopped talking. Dona Sinhá, who baked bread for everyone, began hiding food under her bed. The children stopped playing.

\noindent When the food ran out for good, no one said goodbye.

\noindent We simply left.

\noindent My mother packed a sack with three changes of clothes, a gourd bowl, and an image of Our Lady of Sorrows that had belonged to her grandmother. My father packed nothing. He looked at the house one last time. The open door. The empty table.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Joaquim\textquotedblright{}\textit{, he said. }\textquotedblleft{}Look ahead. Don't look back.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent I looked back.

\noindent The house was burning.

\noindent It was not real fire. It was the light of the setting sun catching the thatch roof and making the house look like flames. It seemed the whole Sertão was on fire.

\noindent I thought: \textit{we are not coming back.}

\noindent We never came back.
