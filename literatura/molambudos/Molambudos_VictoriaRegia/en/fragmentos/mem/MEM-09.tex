\section*{\textbf{MEM-09} --- The Early Years}
\markboth{MEM-09 --- The Early Years}{}

\noindent \textit{The boy who did not speak}

\noindent \textbf{1920-1930 --- The boy who did not speak}

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent In the first years at the Colony, I did not speak.

\noindent Not because I could not. Because it was not worth it.

\noindent Speaking meant answering. Answering meant explaining. Explaining meant telling the story again. And the story --- the trench, the creature, the yellow eye --- no one would understand.

\noindent So I stayed quiet.

\noindent The orderlies liked quiet patients. A quiet patient makes no trouble. A quiet patient takes no time. A quiet patient is like a piece of furniture --- it is there, it takes up space, but it demands nothing.

\noindent I was a piece of furniture.

\noindent Sitting in the corner of the yard. Staring at the ground. All day. Every day.

\noindent The creature slept inside me. Every now and then she would wake up, stretch, and I would feel a pang in my stomach. She was growing.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}You need to eat more\textquotedblright{}, she said.}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}There is no food.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}There is. You just don't see it.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Where?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}In the others.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent I did not answer.

\noindent But I understood.

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent In 1922, I killed Wagner.

\noindent After that, the orderlies began to treat me differently. They did not come close. They left the food at the cell door. They avoided looking into my eyes.

\noindent The isolation cell became my home.

\noindent A cell three meters by two. An iron bed. A bucket. A barred window facing the corridor. I stayed there, sitting, writing in the diary.

\noindent The hours passed.

\noindent The days passed.

\noindent The years passed.

\noindent I lost track of time. The diary became my clock. Each page turned was a day. Each page written was a night.

\noindent The creature spoke more and more.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Write.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}About what?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}About everything. About the trench. About the pen. About the hunger. Write so that someone will read.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Who will read it?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Someone. One day. Someone will open this diary and will see.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}See what?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}The truth.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}What truth?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}The truth no one wants to see. That the world is a trench. That we are born, go hungry, die, and are forgotten. But the diary does not forget. The diary keeps.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent And I wrote.

\noindent Page after page.

\noindent Year after year.

\noindent I wrote until my fingers bled.

\noindent I wrote until the ink ran out --- and then I wrote with blood.

\noindent The diary grew with me.

\noindent It knew things I did not even know I knew.

\noindent Sometimes I would open it to an old page and not recognize the handwriting. I thought it belonged to someone else. But it was mine. My hand. My script.

\noindent But the words...

\noindent The words were not mine.

\noindent They were hers.
