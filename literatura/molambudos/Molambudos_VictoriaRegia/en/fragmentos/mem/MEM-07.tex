\section*{\textbf{MEM-07} --- The Mother Taken}
\markboth{MEM-07 --- The Mother Taken}{}

\noindent \textit{Soldiers, truck, silence}

\noindent \textbf{1915 --- The Government Pen}

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent After the night at the trench, my mother was different.

\noindent She looked at me in a way I did not know. It was not a mother's look --- it was the look of someone who has seen an animal and does not know if the animal is dangerous.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Your eye\textquotedblright{}\textit{, she kept saying. }\textquotedblleft{}Your eye, Joaquim.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent The right eye was yellow. Not pale yellow. Egg yellow. The yellow of rotten yolk. I could see the reflection in the broken crockery we used to drink water.

\noindent I said it was nothing.

\noindent She knew it was a lie.

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent The soldiers came one afternoon.

\noindent There were four of them. Two with rifles. One with a whip. One with a list of names.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Women\textquotedblright{}\textit{, shouted the one with the whip. }\textquotedblleft{}All women between fifteen and forty. Report to the entrance.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent My mother pressed me against her chest.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Stay quiet\textquotedblright{}\textit{, she whispered. }\textquotedblleft{}Stay quiet, and they won't take me.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent But they took her.

\noindent The soldier with the list read her name. She did not answer. He read it again. She did not answer. He came over to us.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Are you Maria do Rosário Correia?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent She did not answer.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}You're on the list. Come.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}My son\textquotedblright{}\textit{, she said. }\textquotedblleft{}My son needs me.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Your son will be fine. The boys stay. The women go to another town. There's food there.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Liar\textquotedblright{}\textit{, she said. }\textquotedblleft{}I know what you do to women.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent The soldier did not answer. He nodded. The other two came over. One held her left arm. The other held her right.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Mother!\textquotedblright{}}, I screamed.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Stay, Joaquim! Stay!\textquotedblright{}}, she screamed back.

\noindent She looked at me. Her eyes were full of water. But it was not crying. It was rage. A rage I had never seen.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Don't die\textquotedblright{}\textit{, she said. }\textquotedblleft{}Don't die, Joaquim. Listen to your mother: don't die.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent The soldiers dragged her away.

\noindent I ran after her.

\noindent The one with the whip shoved me. I fell to the ground. I got up and ran again. He shoved me again. This time I did not get up.

\noindent I stayed on the ground, watching.

\noindent The truck drove away.

\noindent My mother was in the back, among other women. She was looking at me. I was looking at her.

\noindent The truck grew small.

\noindent Then it disappeared.

\noindent I never saw my mother again.\NE{The \textquotedblleft{}relocation\textquotedblright{} and Maria's fate are fictional elements. Violence suffered by retirante women requires specific historical documentation; because Joaquim never existed, his narrative is neither testimony nor confirmation of an official archive. The conflict between the promise of food and the fear of separation drives this fragment only within the fiction.}

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent Old Anselmo, who had not died yet back then, told me later:

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}They go to the brothels on the coast. The government calls it 'relocation'. Those who don't want to go, go into the bush with a bullet in the back of the head.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}And my mother?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Your mother went.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Went where?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent Anselmo spat on the ground.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Went to live. Or to die. In the end it's the same thing.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent I lay down on the ground of the pen. I looked at the sky. The sky was clear. No cloud. No rain. Nothing.

\noindent Only blue.

\noindent A blue that hurt.

\noindent I felt the creature stir inside me.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Calm down\textquotedblright{}\textit{, she said. }\textquotedblleft{}One day you will understand.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Understand what?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}That the world eats its children. And the children learn to eat back.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent I closed my eyes.

\noindent The yellow eye did not close.
