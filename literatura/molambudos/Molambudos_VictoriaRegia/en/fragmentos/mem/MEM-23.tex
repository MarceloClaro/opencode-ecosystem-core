\section*{\textbf{MEM-23} --- The Snow of Ashes}
\markboth{MEM-23 --- The Snow of Ashes}{}

\noindent \textit{The fire of 1960}

\noindent \textbf{1960 --- The fire at the Colony}

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent In 1960, a fire destroyed the administrative wing of the Colony.

\noindent No one died --- the fire started during the day, the staff got out in time. But the patients smelled the smoke and panicked. Shouting. Running. The orderlies locked the cell doors and left.

\noindent We were locked inside, with the smoke coming in through the windows.

\noindent My heart beat harder than the other patients' fists on the iron door. I felt fear --- not of the fire, but of being forgotten in there, the way we had always been forgotten. I did not scream. I chose to save the air for breathing, not to spend it on a scream nobody would hear.

\noindent I looked through the bars.

\noindent The sky was gray. Not from clouds --- from smoke. Pieces of burnt paper flew through the air. It looked like black snow.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}The world is burning\textquotedblright{}}, said the creature.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}It is just a fire.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}No. The world is burning. And you are watching.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}What does that mean?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}It means that everything that is built can be burned. The Colony. The government. The world. It all turns to ashes.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}And what is left?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}What does not burn.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}What does not burn?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent She laughed.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Hunger. Hunger never burns.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent The fire was brought under control in the afternoon.

\noindent The next day, the administrative wing was nothing but ashes.

\noindent But the patients' ward was still standing.

\noindent As always.
As it always will.

\noindent It was on that day that I understood something it took me the rest of my life to admit: the hunger the creature spoke of was not only hers. It was mine as well. And I could no longer say, with any certainty, where mine ended and hers began.
