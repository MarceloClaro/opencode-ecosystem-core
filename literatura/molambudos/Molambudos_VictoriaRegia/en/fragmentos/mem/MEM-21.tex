\section*{\textbf{MEM-21} --- The Newspaper}
\markboth{MEM-21 --- The Newspaper}{}

\noindent \textit{1948 --- the news that lied}

\noindent \textbf{1948 --- A piece of news from the outside}

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent An orderly left a newspaper in the cell. I do not know why. Perhaps forgetfulness. Perhaps pity. The newspaper was from Belo Horizonte, from 1947. A piece of news on page 5 stopped me cold:

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}BARBACENA COLONY TURNS 40 WITH 5,000 INTERNEES\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent I read the article. It said the Colony was \textquotedblleft{}one of the largest psychiatric hospitals in Latin America\textquotedblright{}. It said the government was investing in \textquotedblleft{}modern therapies\textquotedblright{}. It said the patients \textquotedblleft{}received humane treatment\textquotedblright{}.

\noindent A lie.

\noindent All of it a lie.

\noindent I was there. I saw the electroshocks without anesthesia. I saw the patients dying of pneumonia with no medicine. I saw the bodies stacked on the back of the truck every morning.

\noindent The newspaper lied.

\noindent And I thought: \textit{\textquotedblleft{}If the newspaper lies about this, what else does it lie about?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent The creature answered:

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}He lies about everything. Newspapers always lie. They say the world is orderly. It is not. The world is a trench. And the trench is full of bodies the government does not want you to see.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}It lies about everything. Newspapers always lie. They say the world is orderly. It is not. The world is a trench. And the trench is full of bodies the government does not want you to see.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Why are you telling me this?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Because you are the proof. You are alive. You saw. You can tell.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Who will believe me?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Whoever reads the diary.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent She was right.

\noindent The newspaper lied.
The diary did not.

\noindent The diary is the truth the newspaper never published.

\noindent I kept that page of the newspaper inside the mattress, together with the first notebooks of the diary. Not to prove that it lied. To remember, whenever I doubted myself, that I invented nothing of what I saw.
