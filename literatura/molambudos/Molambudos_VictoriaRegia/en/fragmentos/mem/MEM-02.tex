\section*{\textbf{MEM-02} --- The Retirantes' March}
\markboth{MEM-02 --- The Retirantes' March}{}

\noindent \textit{1915 --- The road from Senador Pompeu to Fortaleza}

\noindent \textbf{1915 --- The road from Senador Pompeu to Fortaleza}

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent After months of retreat from Quixeramobim, we reached Senador Pompeu. In 1915, we left from there toward Fortaleza, following the rumor that food could still be found on the coast.

\noindent On the first day, we walked until the sun went down.

\noindent My father in front. My mother behind, with the sack of clothes on her head. Me in the middle, holding the hem of her skirt. The ground was dry and cracked, and every step raised a little puff of dust that got into your nose and left your mouth tasting of earth.

\noindent Every now and then we passed a house. The doors were open. The windows too. There was no one inside. The houses stood empty like cicada shells.

\noindent On the second day, we began to meet the others.

\noindent They came from everywhere. Whole families. People alone. Old men carrying children. Children carrying old men. Everyone walking in the same direction.

\noindent No one spoke.

\noindent Speaking wastes saliva. Saliva needs water. There was no water.

\noindent We walked.

\noindent On the third day, the line was so long I could not see its end. The road was full of people moving slowly, like a river of mud. People stumbled into one another. Some fell and did not get up. We stepped over them.

\noindent My mother said: \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Don't look, Joaquim.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent I looked.

\noindent On the fourth day, my father began to walk differently. His steps grew shorter. His breathing grew louder. He stopped looking ahead. He looked at the ground, as if the ground had an answer.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Father\textquotedblright{}\textit{, I said. }\textquotedblleft{}Father, I want water.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent He did not answer.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Father.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent He stopped. Turned his face. His mouth was open. His tongue was white. His eyes were red.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Father?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent He tried to speak. No sound came out. Only air. Air that smelled of an empty stomach.

\noindent He sat down on the ground.

\noindent My mother sat down beside him.

\noindent I sat down beside her.

\noindent The line of retirantes passed us by. No one stopped. No one asked. Everyone was busy dying.

\noindent On the fifth day, he stopped beneath a dead juazeiro tree.

\noindent My mother knelt in front of him. They looked at each other. She said nothing. Neither did he. He only shook his head.

\noindent She stood up. Took my hand.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Come, Joaquim.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent I looked back. He was still sitting there. Head fallen. Arm stretched out on his lap.

\noindent My mother pulled my hand.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Come.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent We kept walking.

\noindent The line in front. The line behind.

\noindent Me in the middle.

\noindent Learning that the world is a place where people are left behind and we keep walking.
