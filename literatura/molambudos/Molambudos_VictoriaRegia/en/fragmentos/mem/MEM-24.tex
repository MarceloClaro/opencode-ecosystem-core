\section*{\textbf{MEM-24} --- The Nurse Who Cried}
\markboth{MEM-24 --- The Nurse Who Cried}{}

\noindent \textit{Small kindnesses}

\noindent \textbf{1964 --- Small kindnesses}

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent Every now and then, a new nurse would appear.

\noindent They came from the countryside, girls from poor families who needed work. They entered the Colony without knowing what it was. They left within a week --- or they stayed and hardened.

\noindent One of them, in 1964, was different.

\noindent Her name was Isabel. She was 19. Black hair, big eyes, hands that trembled when she handed out the food.

\noindent In her first week, she passed in front of my cell and saw the diary.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}What do you write, sir?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}The truth.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent She did not understand. But she kept passing by.

\noindent Every afternoon, she brought the food. While the other orderlies threw the plate through the hole in the door, she opened the door and handed it to me by hand.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}You are not dangerous, sir\textquotedblright{}, she said one day.}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}How do you know?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Dangerous people do not write. Dangerous people do not think. You write and you think. You are only sad.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Sad?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent She lowered her eyes.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}You have the saddest eye I have ever seen.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}It is yellow.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent She shook her head.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Yellow is the color. Sad is what is behind it.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent She left.

\noindent That night, the creature spoke:

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}She likes you.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Shut up.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}She wants to save you.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}No one saves anyone here.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}She wants to try.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}...She will leave.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}How do you know?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}Everyone leaves. Everyone who comes in here, gets out. On their feet. Or in a coffin.\textquotedblright{}}

\vspace{0.5em}\noindent\rule{\textwidth}{0.5pt}\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent Isabel stayed for three months.

\noindent One day, she did not come anymore.

\noindent The orderly who brought the food said: \textit{\textquotedblleft{}She quit. She said she could not stand to see.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent I asked: \textit{\textquotedblleft{}See what?\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent He looked at me.

\noindent \textit{\textquotedblleft{}See you all.\textquotedblright{}}

\noindent After Isabel left, I closed the diary for a week.

\noindent Not for lack of words.
For lack of hope.

\noindent If even those who wanted to help went away, then nothing was worth it.

\noindent The Colony was not a hospital.
It was a hole.
And everything that fell into the hole --- was forgotten.
